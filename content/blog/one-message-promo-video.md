The most common thing we hear at the start of a video project is a list: "Can it also mention the integrations, the pricing, the new dashboard and the support team?" Every item on that list is true and important. Every item also makes the film weaker. A promo video has one job, which is to leave one idea in someone's head, and the rule we build every film around is simple.

> One promise, to one kind of person, with one action.

## Why one message sells more than five

A promo is not a brochure. It plays for a few seconds in a feed, on a landing page or at the start of a sales call, usually while the viewer is half doing something else.

- **Attention is short.** Each extra idea takes time away from the one that matters.
- **Memory is shorter.** People leave an ad remembering one thing at most. If you don't choose it, they will choose for you, and it may be your logo colour.
- **Action needs a single path.** One message leads to one obvious next step. Five messages lead to "I'll look later".

Everything else you want to say still has a home: your website, your sales deck, your onboarding. The film's job is to make people want to see those things.

## How to find your one message

1. **Write down everything you want to say.** All of it. Getting it out of your head makes the next step easier.
2. **Circle the one thing a customer would pay for.** Not the feature you are proudest of. The result they are buying.
3. **Write it as an outcome, not a feature.** "The right tech. Every job." beats "AI-powered scheduling engine".
4. **Cut it to eight words or fewer.** If it doesn't fit on a phone screen in two lines, it is still two messages.
5. **Run the competitor test.** Could a competitor put the same sentence in their film? If yes, sharpen it until only you could say it.

## Everything else serves the message

Once you have the message, every other decision gets easier, because each element only has to answer one question: does this help the one message land?

| Element | Its job | Keep it if | Cut it if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Make the right person stop | It names the problem your message solves | It is clever but about something else |
| Product shot | Prove the promise | It shows the one feature behind the message | It is a tour of every screen |
| On-screen text | Carry the story with the sound off | One short line per beat | It needs pausing to read |
| Music and sound | Set the pace and feeling | It matches your brand's tone | It fights the text for attention |
| End card | Ask for one action | It has one button or one next step | It lists three ways to get in touch |

For a beat-by-beat example of this in a finished film, read our [shot-by-shot breakdown of the Anywhere film](/blog/anatomy-of-a-15-second-promo-film). For the opening line specifically, see [six hooks that stop the scroll](/blog/video-hooks-that-stop-the-scroll).

## What to have ready before you brief a video

You don't need a script to start a video project. You do need a few decisions, because the film can only be as clear as the brief.

- [ ] Your one message, in one sentence
- [ ] Who the film is for (one kind of person)
- [ ] The one action you want viewers to take
- [ ] Where the film will run: social feeds, your website, sales calls
- [ ] Your brand assets: logo, colours, fonts and product screens
- [ ] Your deadline

If you only have the first two, that is fine. Finding the message is part of the work, and it is the part we enjoy most.

## The short version

One promise, to one kind of person, with one action. Write it down before anything else, test it against your competitors, and let every frame serve it. Stuck choosing between five good messages? [Start a video project](/video) and bring the list. Finding the one is part of the work.
