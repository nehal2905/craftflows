Most of the promo films we make are watched on a phone held upright. Designing for that from the first frame, rather than cropping a widescreen film at the end, is the difference between a video that feels native to the feed and one that looks like an ad squeezed into it. This is the checklist we work through on every vertical film.

## The specs we use

| Setting | What we use |
| --- | --- |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16, vertical |
| Resolution | 1080 × 1920 pixels |
| Frame rate | 30 or 60 fps. Our promo films run at 60 for smooth motion graphics |
| File | MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio |
| Length | 6 to 20 seconds for social ads, up to about 60 seconds for launch cuts |
| Captions | Burned into the picture, plus a separate caption file |

Each platform publishes its own upload limits and changes them from time to time, so check the current numbers before a big campaign. The settings above are a safe default that every major vertical feed accepts.

## Safe zones: where the app covers your film

Reels, TikTok and Shorts all draw their own interface on top of your video: the account name and caption along the bottom, the like, comment and share buttons down the right, and sometimes a header or search bar at the top. Anything important you put in those areas can end up hidden.

Our rules of thumb:

- **Keep headlines and the product in the middle band** of the frame, as in the Onto frame above.
- **Leave roughly the bottom fifth clear** of anything people need to read.
- **Keep a margin on the right** for the stack of buttons.
- **Centre the end card.** Put the logo and the call to action in the middle, not in a corner.

Exact zones vary by app and by device, which is why we design to a generous safe area instead of chasing each platform's pixel guides.

## Text that reads on a phone

On-screen text carries the story in a vertical film, so it has to be readable in a glance.

1. **One idea per screen.** A short line per beat. If a line needs a pause to read, it is two lines.
2. **Big and bold.** If you can't read it on your own phone at arm's length, it is too small.
3. **High contrast.** Light text on dark or dark text on light. Never thin text over busy footage without something behind it.
4. **On screen long enough to read twice.** Viewers read slower than editors think.

## Design for sound off

Plenty of people scroll with the sound off, so we write every film to make sense without audio. Every line of the story is on screen as text, the product is shown rather than described, and music and sound effects are a reward for the people who have their sound on. If a film has a voiceover, its captions are burned in, not left to the app.

> If your film only makes sense with the sound on, it only works for half your audience.

## One master film, every format

A vertical promo rarely lives in one place. The same film often needs to run in feeds, on your website and in sales decks, each with a different shape. We plan for that before the first frame is designed.

| Format | Ratio | Where it runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical | 9:16 | Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Stories |
| Portrait | 4:5 | Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn feeds |
| Square | 1:1 | Feeds and marketplaces |
| Widescreen | 16:9 | Your website, YouTube, sales decks |

**Keep the important things near the centre.** If the headline and product sit in the middle of the 9:16 frame, the 4:5 and 1:1 crops keep them without a redesign.

**Design the widescreen version on purpose.** A vertical film placed in the middle of a 16:9 frame with black bars on each side looks like an afterthought. We rearrange the layout for widescreen so it uses the full width.

**Name your files clearly.** Something like `brand-film_9x16_15s.mp4` saves whoever uploads it from guessing.

## Export checklist

- [ ] 1080 × 1920, 9:16
- [ ] H.264 MP4 at 30 or 60 fps
- [ ] Captions burned in, plus a separate caption file
- [ ] Headline, product and call to action inside the safe area
- [ ] A first frame that works as the thumbnail
- [ ] An end card with your logo and one action
- [ ] Every format exported and clearly named

## The short version

Design vertical first, keep the story in the middle of the frame, make every line work with the sound off, and plan your crops before you design.
