Fifteen seconds sounds like nothing. In a promo film it is plenty, as long as every second has a job. There is no room for a warm-up, a slow logo reveal or a list of features. Here is how our Anywhere film is built, beat by beat, and how you can use the same structure for your own product.

## The five beats at a glance

Anywhere puts your places, plans and memories on one 3D map. The film has to make that idea land in the time it takes to scroll past it. It does it in five beats.

| Beat | Time | Its job | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | 0 to 3 s | Make the viewer nod | "Your places, plans and memories live in different apps." |
| Turn | 3 to 7 s | Introduce the idea | "All on one living map." |
| Proof | 7 to 10 s | Show it working | "Connected to your agent." |
| Payoff | 10 to 13 s | Name the outcome | "Your only map." |
| Brand | 13 to 15 s | Ask for one action | "Anywhere. Download on the App Store." |

> Every beat answers the one before it. That is what makes fifteen seconds feel like a story instead of an ad.

## What we left out on purpose

A short film is mostly decisions about what not to show. In this one:

- **No feature list.** The app does more than one thing. The film shows one.
- **No logo at the start.** A logo in the first second tells people it is an ad before they have a reason to care.
- **No voiceover.** Every line works with the sound off. Music and sound effects reward the people who have it on.
- **No decoration.** Every transition moves the story forward. If a shot did not change what the viewer understood, it was cut.

## Fit your product into the five beats

You can use this structure for almost any product. Here it is as a fill-in template.

<div class="callout">
<p class="callout__label">The five-beat template</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Problem:</strong> one sentence your customer would say about their day. Start with "Your" or with who they are.</li>
<li><strong>Turn:</strong> your product's idea in six words or fewer.</li>
<li><strong>Proof:</strong> one feature, shown working in your real product.</li>
<li><strong>Payoff:</strong> the problem sentence, flipped into the outcome.</li>
<li><strong>Brand:</strong> your name, your line, and one clear action.</li>
</ol>
</div>

Here is the same structure in two of our other films, so you can see it works outside travel.

| Beat | Coach (sales coaching) | Fleety (field service dispatch) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | "Managers can't be everywhere." | "A job cancels. A tech runs late. An urgent call comes in." |
| Turn | "Every rep gets a personal AI coach." | "Fleety rebuilds the schedule all day." |
| Payoff | "Train every rep like your top performer." | "The right tech. Every job." |

## Mistakes that sink short promos

1. **Three messages in fifteen seconds.** The viewer remembers none of them. Pick one. We wrote a whole guide on [the one-message rule](/blog/one-message-promo-video).
2. **Stock footage instead of the product.** People buy what they can see. Show your real screens, your real product, your real colours.
3. **Text too small or too long.** If you cannot read a line on your phone at arm's length in about a second, it is too small or too long.
4. **A weak first frame.** The opening line does most of the work. Here are [six hooks that stop the scroll](/blog/video-hooks-that-stop-the-scroll).
5. **A vague ending.** "Learn more" is not an action. "Download on the App Store" is.

## The short version

Problem, turn, proof, payoff, brand. Each beat answers the one before it, and nothing stays on screen unless it moves the story forward. Run your own product through the template above, then see the same structure across other industries in [our films](/work).
